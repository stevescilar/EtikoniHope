What We Do

Holistic Youth Development & Transparent Action

In Etikoni, children and youth face multifaceted challenges that cannot be solved by money alone. We combine education restoration, spiritual mentoring, life skills, and nutritional security to build lasting resilience.

Our Shared Commitment

The Three Pillars of Our Action Plan

“The training and equipping of these children happens to make sure our end result is well achieved: serving God through touching a child and strengthening the family.”

1

Create Safe Spaces

We provide welcoming physical sanctuaries at JCC Etikoni where youth are heard, validated, shielded from domestic violence, and surrounded by mentors who believe in them.

2

Holistic Growth

Supporting programs that help them grow across all 4 key dimensions: spiritually in God's Word, cognitively in schooling, socioemotionally in friendships, and physically in health.

3

Champion Their Future

We advocate vigorously for their schooling, celebrate their milestones from primary to university, and help them break inherited patterns of generational poverty.

Program Area 01

Education Restoration & Academic Sponsorship

In Kenya, tuition subsidies alone do not keep rural children in school. Parents earning KSh 150 a day cannot buy required uniforms, personal textbooks, exercise books, or pay PTA building levies.

Etikoni Hope identifies out-of-school dropouts and re-enrolls them. We coordinate with local headteachers in Mbiuni and Mwala, supply essential learning materials, and monitor attendance to ensure no child slips through the cracks.

  • Provision of sturdy school shoes and custom uniforms
  • Exercise books, geometry sets, and primary/secondary textbooks
  • Secondary school & technical college transition sponsorship

Program Area 02

Spiritual Discipleship, Awana & Mentorship

Idle youth face severe temptations in rural areas: drug abuse, early pregnancies, premature marriages, and negative cultural fatalism. We provide an inspiring community alternative.

Through Awana youth clubs, outdoor nature hikes up the Machakos hills, choir ministry, and dedicated one-on-one sessions with mature Christian leaders, our youth discover their identity as precious children of God.

  • Awana Scripture memorization and games clubs
  • Life skills workshops: drug resistance, hygiene, and reproductive health
  • Group hikes fostering physical endurance, camaraderie, and joy
Accountability & Fairness

Transparent Selection Criteria

To ensure donor funds reach the genuine “poor of the poor” without bias or favoritism, JCC Etikoni and Etikoni Hope use a verifiable 9-point assessment conducted directly in the village.

01

Orphaned & Vulnerable

A vulnerable child who has lost one or both parents and lacks adequate guardian support.

02

Extreme Low Income

Child of a single parent whose monthly earnings are KSh 3,000 (~3 USD) or below.

03

Elderly / Ill Caregivers

A child living with elderly grandparents or chronically ill guardians unable to engage in manual labor.

04

Food & Shelter Deprivation

A family that cannot afford at least two meals a day, wearing torn clothes, and sleeping in unstable shelter.

05

Frequent School Absenteeism

A child repeatedly sent home from school due to lack of books, uniform, or PTA contributions.

06

Unsafe Sanitation Conditions

Homesteads lacking basic sanitary toilets, clean drinking water, or essential health hygiene.

07

Traditional & Cultural Barriers

Children denied schooling due to traditional superstition, early marriage pressures, or ritual practices.

08

Exposed to Child Labor

Minors forced into quarry work, sand harvesting, or domestic servitude to support the family table.

09

Abandoned or Neglected

Youth abandoned by caregivers who lack adult oversight, stable lodging, and emotional guidance.

Physical Well-Being

“Good Health in a Child is Very Key”

Illness and untreated communicable diseases steal school days and impair brain development. In our community, poor diets and recurring droughts make young children vulnerable to stunting and infections.

Etikoni Hope conducts periodic health checkups for enrolled children and provides nutritional safety nets during critical drought seasons. When a child is healthy and nourished, their ability to concentrate and learn quadruples.
